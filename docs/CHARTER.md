# Charter: the hard lines

These don't bend for a vote, a sponsor or a good week. A pull request that breaks one is closed.

## 1. No money, ever
- The Flock never takes deposits, payments, subscriptions or donations "to invest".
- It never pools money, never trades for anyone and never links to a broker, exchange, wallet or app.
- No paid tier, no "VIP" group, no DMs. Anyone who says they represent The Flock and asks for money is a scammer.

## 2. Research, not advice
- The portfolio is a **paper model** ($100,000 notional). It is the same for every reader and knows nothing about
  anyone's situation. Nothing here says what any person should buy or sell.
- Every page carries the line: "Open source. Not investment advice. A collaboration of thought."
- Results are shown honestly: every trade, every loss, and the comparison with a plain index fund (VTI), even when
  the fund wins.

## 3. Public information only
- Sources must be public: filings (10-K, 10-Q, 8-K), earnings calls, government data, published research, news.
- "Insider knowledge" here means **what insiders must disclose publicly**: SEC Form 4 insider buys and sells,
  13F fund holdings, and congressional trade disclosures. Never non-public information. A submission that claims a
  private tip is rejected, whatever it says.
- Contributions must link the public sources they use, so a reviewer can open them. No invented numbers or quotes.

## 4. Built so it can't be pumped
- Fixed rules set the weights, never popularity ([METHODOLOGY.md](METHODOLOGY.md)). If a public vote is ever added, it
  picks what gets researched, never what is held.
- Only large, liquid holdings: stocks over $10B market cap, or broad, liquid ETFs (including gold and a bitcoin fund). No penny stocks, no
  memecoins, no single-name options, no leverage, no shorting.
- At most 5% in one company, 25% in one sector, and the focused bets together never more than 35%. Changes happen only when the research changes, each logged with the reason.

## Conflicts
The maintainers also use this research for their own investing, and may hold some of the same funds or stocks. To
keep that clean:
- A maintainer doesn't trade a name in the 3 trading days before or after a portfolio change in that name is
  published. The research is public first.
- No one is paid by any company the portfolio holds or researches. Sponsorship, if any, is for Swarm as a whole and
  never steers research.

## Anything that takes input
The site today is read-only: plain files, no forms, no accounts, no cookies, no outside requests. Before anything
that takes input is added (a vote, a form), it gets an independent security review first.
